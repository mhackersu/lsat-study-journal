# Skill
Understanding the passage is the most critical step in LR. Restate each sentence in your own words so it makes sense to you. If a sentence doesn’t make sense, stop and reread it until it does.

As you study, you’ll discover logical flaws in your thinking. Fixing these flaws will help your score, but learning to read carefully will help it even more. You’re more likely to miss questions from misreading than from faulty reasoning.

# Question
What should you do when you don’t understand a sentence?

A
Move on to the next sentence 

B
Go back to the beginning of the passage

*C
Reread the sentence

D
Look for a similar sentence in the answer choices 

E
Skip to the next question

# Explanation
The key to getting better at reading is practice. And you can’t practice if you skim, skip, or submit to the very sentences that give you trouble. They are your golden tickets to pushing your skills further. Take every opportunity to practice crushing those juggernauts by rereading them until you do.

A
The next sentence will rarely clarify the one you’re stuck on. You must be disciplined and sit with the sentence until it makes sense. To be clear, you can read the next sentence for more context—but only after trying to unlock the previous sentence first. Once you have that context, go back and read that previous sentence.

B
Starting over and rereading the entire passage is a last resort.

C
Sit in the discomfort. Do your best to figure out what the sentence is saying. Try to restate the sentence in your own words or think of a specific example to help yourself visualize the idea.

D
The answer choices are never there to help you.

E
You are stronger, smarter, and better than the LSAT. Don’t give up. You got this.
